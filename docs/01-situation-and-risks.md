# Situation and risks (read before anything else)

> Prep notes, not professional advice. Verify against IRS/CRA pages and confirm with a cross-border CPA.

## 1. The big correction to the current plan
The plan was: *US return for salaries; Canadian return for rental + dividends.* That is probably incomplete:

- **US tax residency**: TN status does not exempt anyone from the substantial presence test (only F/J/M/Q students/teachers are exempt). If you are in the US most of the year, you are very likely a **US resident alien** and the US taxes **worldwide income**, including the **Canadian rental income and Canadian dividends**. These go on the 1040 (Schedule E, Schedule B), with a **foreign tax credit (Form 1116)** for Canadian tax paid.
- **Canadian residency**: with a spouse and home in the US you have likely become a **non-resident of Canada** (or a deemed non-resident via the Canada–US treaty tie-breaker). That changes the Canadian side from a normal T1 to non-resident rules (below). If you never formally left (no departure filing), clarify this with a CPA – it affects the "departure tax" on the corporation shares.

## 2. Canadian side (as a non-resident)
| Item | Typical treatment | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Rental income | 25% Part XIII withholding on **gross** rent, unless you file **NR6** before the year's first rent to withhold on net. Then file a **section 216 return** (T1159) by **June 30** following the year. | NR6 each year; NR4 slips from agent/tenant; s.216 return |
| Dividends from Canadian corp | Part XIII withholding 25%, reduced to **15% by treaty**; the corporation withholds, remits and issues **NR4**. This is generally a final tax; no T1 needed for it. | Confirm the corp actually withheld/remitted and filed the NR4; if not, fix it (corp is liable) |
| Whose name for rental | Taxed to the **legal owner(s) in proportion to ownership**. Moving it to the wife for tax reasons without a real ownership change is not allowed, and a transfer can trigger tax and attribution rules. | Check title; decide with CPA |
| Ontario corporation (next year) | Corporate T2 return, corporate tax filings, shareholder compensation, NR4s. | See §4 |

## 3. US side – extra forms triggered by foreign assets (often missed, big penalties)
- **FBAR (FinCEN 114)** – if combined foreign account balances (incl. signing authority over the **corporation's bank account**) exceeded $10,000 at any time.
- **Form 8938 (FATCA)** – higher thresholds; foreign accounts and foreign corporation shares.
- **Form 5471** – if you own ≥10% of a foreign (Canadian) corporation as a US person. Penalty starts at $10,000 per form per year. Also triggers CFC rules (Subpart F/GILTI) possibly taxing the corp's income on you before dividends are paid, and corporation-tax-rate/treaty issues.
- **Form 1116** – foreign tax credit for Canadian tax on rent/dividends.
- **Canadian registered accounts**: TFSA/RESP/FHSA are generally not tax-free in the US and may need Form 3520/3520-A; RRSP/RRIF get treaty deferral without a form since Rev. Proc. 2014-55 but still FBAR/8938.
- **Rental depreciation**: US requires depreciation (27.5 yrs for residential rental, in USD) on the Canadian property – different from Canadian CCA; do not claim CCA in Canada if you want to avoid recapture issues.
- **Married filing jointly**: if both spouses are US residents you can file MFJ; income of both and the foreign items combine.

## 4. Immigration risk for the Ontario corporation (flag early!)
A TN allows work only for the sponsoring US employer in the listed profession. Actively working in/for your own corporation (even in Canada vs. for clients) can raise status concerns; passive ownership is different. **Consult an immigration attorney before incorporating or working for it.** Also determine whether the existing "business" is an incorporated company and whether you actively run it.

## 5. Consequences of getting this wrong (why it's worth a CPA)
Missing 5471 / FBAR / 8938 can mean five-figure penalties. A cross-border CPA/EA for the first year plus ongoing use of this repo for prep is the recommended approach.
